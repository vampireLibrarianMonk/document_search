# Roofing Repair Test Questions

## Test Request 1: New Roofer Outreach

Create a plain-language email to a new roofer who is coming to inspect my roof.

Use the roofing inspection documents and HOA documents in OpenSearch to ground the email.

The email should explain that I live in a four-story townhouse with a flat roof. It should mention the roof age and assessed roof materials from the inspection report. It should summarize the inspection concerns, including the torn or split roof seam, possible leak risk, worn sealant or flashing concerns, and ponding or standing water.

The email should ask the roofer to assess whether the roof is still a good candidate for repair or whether replacement is the smarter path. It should ask about checking under or around the split seam for wet insulation, damaged wood, rotten wood, or soft spots. It should ask how extra wood or insulation replacement costs are usually calculated. It should also ask whether the roofer can provide before, during, and after photos if work is performed.

The email should also explain that the repair area may involve the neighboring townhouse, shared seam area, party wall area, roof edge, flashing, metal cap, or other shared exterior condition. Ask the roofer to clearly separate what work applies only to my property, what work applies to the neighbor's property, and what work is shared. Ask whether they recommend one combined repair or separate repairs. Ask how billing would be separated between me and the neighbor, whether the invoice can clearly separate each party's portion, and whether written permission from the neighbor is needed before touching any area connected to their roof or exterior.

Do not include the back gutter extension or rear downspout issue.

Use layman's terms, avoid legalistic language, avoid em dashes, and make the email ready to send.

## Test Request 2: Follow-up to American Home Contractors

Create a plain-language follow-up email to American Home Contractors about their roof repair estimate.

Use the roofing proposal from American Home Contractors, the home inspection report, and the HOA exterior modification application documents in OpenSearch to ground the email.

The email should reference that their estimate discusses multiple EPDM rubber roof areas needing repair to avoid water leaks, cleaning affected seam areas, using sealant, installing patches or flashing, patching an existing hole, and matching the black EPDM rubber and almond metal apron flashing.

The email should ask them to confirm whether their repair addresses the torn or split seam found in the inspection, whether it is expected to stop water from getting in or is more temporary, whether they will check under the damaged seam area for wet insulation, damaged wood, rotten wood, or soft spots, and whether any hidden damage is included in the quoted price or would be extra.

The email should also explain that the repair may involve the neighboring townhouse, shared seam area, party wall area, roof edge, flashing, metal cap, or other shared exterior condition. Ask American Home Contractors to clearly separate what work applies only to my property, what work applies to the neighbor's property, and what work is shared. Ask whether they recommend one combined repair or separate repairs. Ask how billing would be separated between me and the neighbor, whether the invoice can clearly separate each party's portion, and whether written permission from the neighbor is needed before touching any area connected to their roof or exterior.

The email should also ask them to provide a short HOA-ready description of the work, including the exact roof areas being repaired, materials, colors, whether the visible appearance will match the existing roof and exterior, whether roof edges, wall edges, metal caps, flashing, or visible trim will be changed, whether staging or equipment will be used, the expected start date and duration, cleanup expectations, and photos or marked-up images showing repair locations.

The HOA-ready description should also state whether any portion of the work affects or touches the neighboring townhouse, and if so, how that work and cost should be separated.

Do not include the back gutter extension or rear downspout issue.

Use layman's terms, avoid legalistic language, avoid em dashes, and make the email ready to send.
